Launching the Ames market

  • Recruiting
  • Onboarding
  • Operations

The situation. UniMovers wanted to expand into Ames and had nobody on the ground there.

What I did. Led the expansion independently — sourcing, interviewing and onboarding a localized team of 7+ student employees, then getting them working. No existing playbook to follow and no local manager above me.

What I learned. Recruiting is a marketing problem. The pitch to a prospective employee has the same structure as the pitch to a customer: who you are, why it is worth their time, and what to do next.

Scope: independent market launch · 7+ hires

HOW I APPROACH IT

The same questions every time

Whether it is a campaign, a hiring push or a web page, the planning questions do not change much. These are the ones from my coursework that have held up against real work.

  1. Step 1 Who is the audience?

    Not demographics for their own sake — what they actually want, and why they are here right now.

  2. Step 2 What is the offer?

    Stated plainly and early. If it takes a paragraph to explain, it is not ready.

  3. Step 3 What should they do next?

    One clear next step per page or per post. More options means fewer decisions.

  4. Step 4 Test it on someone else.

    Give them a task and watch. People will not volunteer that they are confused, but a failed task is not arguable.

  5. Step 5 Change one thing at a time.

    Otherwise you learn that something improved without learning what.
