I run marketing campaigns and build the teams that deliver them

I am Jack Norby, a marketing, entrepreneurship and applied AI student. I have run social media marketing campaigns across four channels for a moving company, recruited the seven-person team that opened a new market in Ames, and spent a summer responsible for a cabin of campers in Colorado.

My marketing work has always run on a small budget, which is a useful constraint. It means every campaign has to earn attention rather than buy it, and every page has to say what it offers before a visitor decides to leave.

What carries across all of it is the same idea: people decide quickly, and they decide on whether something feels clear and trustworthy. That holds just as true for a thumbnail someone scrolls past in half a second as it does for the first screen of a website. So I try to earn the second glance rather than assume it, and to make the next step obvious once I have it.

Boring navigation is good navigation

I built this site by hand for my web development coursework. Good navigation is clear, precise, familiar and consistent, and it is not the place to be creative. The menu is in the same position and order on all four pages, and the logo in the top-left corner returns you home from anywhere.

Visual hierarchy

Larger, higher elements read as more important, on a fixed type scale.

Negative space

Space between elements reduces overload and guides the eye to the next action.

Contrast

Near-black text on white clears WCAG AA, so copy stays legible on a phone.
